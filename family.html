For parents, siblings & partners

Supporting someone who's struggling

Whether it's addiction, anxiety or depression, you can't fix it for them — but how you respond matters. Here's what helps — and what usually doesn't.

What actually helps

1

Talk without judgement

Lead with concern, not accusation. "I'm worried about you" lands better than "you always do this."

2

Set calm, clear boundaries

Boundaries protect the whole family without needing anger — decide them when you're calm, not mid-argument.

3

Encourage professional help

Offer to go with them to a counselor or center — the first step is often the hardest to take alone.

4

Take care of yourself too

Supporting someone is exhausting. Family counseling exists for you as much as for them.

What usually makes it worse

✕

Public shaming or ultimatums in anger

It deepens shame and secrecy instead of opening a conversation.

✕

Covering for them repeatedly

Protecting them from every consequence can delay them seeking help.

✕

Waiting for "rock bottom"

You don't have to wait for a crisis to reach out for support — earlier is always better.
